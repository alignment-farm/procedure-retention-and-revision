**Can a supported correction revise both familiar acquired instances and
unfamiliar applications while preserving complete unaffected behavior through
repeated use?** This phase concerns changing rules and their acquired effects.
S1 separately investigates selection among still-valid experience. Neither study
depends on the other's results or a shared experimental pipeline.

## Starting evidence and broader workload

The accepted [scope findings](FINDINGS-SCOPE.md), publication
`6e7049458732c4f3e37e5b8a3d1993129c2bdcfa`, show that small boundary sets can
establish routing locality. Broader history has conditional effects on complete
calls, and every primary endpoint loses some previously correct unchanged calls.
One treatment leaves all four familiar scoped routes stale despite correcting
most unfamiliar inputs. Excluding obsolete records from replay did not remove
their acquired effects; the supplied examples still permitted an identifier-based
exception. Stronger R4 preservation was not established.

Use that routing task for calibration, then develop at least one procedure with
multiple dependent decisions or resulting-state consequences. A sandbox work-order
process involving prerequisites, eligibility and state changes is one possible
example, not a prescribed task. Choose a procedure whose complete success and
correction consequences can be independently checked. The new contribution should
extend beyond another routing table, with meaningful contact between learned
decisions and subsequent outcomes.

## First causal comparison

Choose one bounded contrast that can distinguish stale acquired instances from
inadequate correction evidence or damage to unaffected behavior. Start paired
treatments from common functioning states, documenting complete-task acquisition
alongside component readiness. Evidence connecting familiar and unfamiliar cases
and contrasting changed with unchanged conditions is a promising intervention;
an explicitly supplied scoped rule is another, with different information access.
Do not require finite examples to determine behavior on every possible input.
Instead, address substantive alternative rules exposed by the actual design,
including the familiar-identifier exception from the preceding study.

Keep correction labels, boundary information, rehearsal and update duration
visible in the comparison. Match the relevant access and budget when attributing
an effect to a method; when information changes, name that intervention. State
whether validity filtering, history relabeling and correction authority are
supplied by the investigator or inferred by the system. Compare with a competent
explicit-memory alternative using maintained examples, records or rules. Disclose
privileged rule information and the work required to construct or revise it.

Measure recall of directly relabeled familiar cases separately from transfer of
the revision. A reusable-correction claim should also evaluate additional formerly
learned instances or withheld combinations involving familiar entities, alongside
new instances and downstream consequences. Pair identical unaffected tasks before
and after changes, reporting complete successes lost and gained as well as
component errors. Correct routing alone cannot establish complete procedural
preservation. A changed target
is a revision obligation, not an unchanged retention case.

## Repeated use and S5 costs

Include recurring use and revisions in the chosen bounded workload. Compare
complete-task quality through that sequence with the competent explicit-memory
reference. Account for initial acquisition, evidence maintenance, rehearsal,
inference and repair, including the cost of identifying obsolete records. Keep
tokens, updates, storage, elapsed time and any monetary costs in their actual
units. Separate the method's deployed costs from experimental search, audits and
investigator effort. Cheaper repeated failures do not establish amortization;
report quality and costs together rather than forcing a single ranking.

This makes S5 an active measurement question within the experiment, without
requiring a large recurrence-by-revision matrix or a new consolidation algorithm.

## Development and publication

Use development material to establish a functioning regime and diagnose acquisition
failures purposefully. Preserve attempts and consequential changes, then test
developed claims on fresh material with selection fixed beforehand. A treatment
win is unnecessary; explanatory progress, a demonstrated limitation or a concrete
resource constraint can close the phase. Coordinate heavy shared-hardware use.
Preserve both accepted publications, the original follow-up brief and frozen
protocols. Publish this phase separately with evidence and reproduction instructions.
